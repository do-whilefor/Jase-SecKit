# Multi-stage business and protocol chains

## Use
Use when acceptance, credential issuance, background processing, status lookup,
or final delivery are different events. This card supplies a method, not
evidence that the stages are connected for the current target.

## Test
Follow the specific input through validation, transformation or state change,
and its final consumer. For each observed stage, locate the actor, input,
output, applicable rule, and evidence. Link stages through actual identifiers
and compatible conditions; shared names or nearby timestamps are only leads.

Retrieve the legitimate flow and any blocked branch. Distinguish a submitted
object from a produced job, credential, or file. Check whether the next consumer
accepts that exact product under the relevant identity, purpose, lifetime, and
deployment. State where the chain is observed and where it is only inferred.

When authorization timing matters, obtain the intended policy and evidence of
the actual execution order. A policy may evaluate authority at submission or at
consumption. Do not invent its expected timing from a response alone.

## Confirm
Support the proposed capability only when evidence connects the necessary
stages through the final business result under compatible conditions. An
intermediate capability may be recorded separately with its remaining gaps.

## Refute
A demonstrated prerequisite failure or sufficiently observed interruption can
refute the particular proposed chain. A later status rejection does not by
itself prove that no earlier restricted processing occurred.

## Unknown
An accepted request, a job ID, or an issued token proves only its observed
stage. Missing execution logs, consumer observations, policy, or correlation
must remain an explicit gap. Choose a focused evidence read or bounded
experiment that distinguishes the competing stage explanations.
